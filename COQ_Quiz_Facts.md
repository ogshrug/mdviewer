# COQ — Quiz Facts (Per Topic, Per Category)

Facts marked **[doc]** are notes already in your original list; the rest are commonly quizzed extra facts for the same topic.

## Movies

**Citizen Kane (1941, Orson Welles)**
- **[doc]** The mansion "Xanadu" belongs to Charles Foster Kane; "Rosebud" is the sled/childhood-innocence clue.
- **[doc]** Modeled loosely on newspaper magnate William Randolph Hearst (Kane = Thatcher's ward).
- Cinematography by Gregg Toland (deep focus, low-angle shots).
- Famously topped Sight & Sound's greatest-films poll for 50 years (dethroned by Vertigo in 2012).

**Christopher Nolan (as director / linking films)**
- **[doc]** Cobb is the lead character in *Inception*.
- **[doc]** *Insomnia* (2002, Nolan, starring Al Pacino/Robin Williams) is a remake of a 1997 Norwegian film starring Stellan Skarsgård.
- Known for practical effects, nonlinear timelines, and IMAX filmmaking (Memento, The Dark Knight, Interstellar, Dunkirk, Oppenheimer).

**The Godfather (1972) / Part II (1974)**
- Directed by Francis Ford Coppola; based on Mario Puzo's novel.
- Part II is one of the few sequels to win Best Picture, and uniquely intercuts a prequel (young Vito) with a sequel (Michael) timeline.

**2001: A Space Odyssey (1968, Stanley Kubrick)**
- **[doc]** The ship's AI is called HAL 9000 (letters each one before IBM in the alphabet — a popular but denied fan theory).
- Based on/expanded with Arthur C. Clarke's writing; famous "Star Gate" sequence and match-cut (bone to satellite).

**Vertigo (1958, Alfred Hitchcock)**
- Introduced the "dolly zoom" (Vertigo effect).
- Topped the 2012 Sight & Sound critics' poll, unseating Citizen Kane.

**Casablanca (1942, Michael Curtiz)**
- Famous (mis-)quoted line "Play it again, Sam" never actually said verbatim in the film.
- Set in Vichy-controlled Morocco during WWII; won Best Picture.

**Seven Samurai (1954, Akira Kurosawa)**
- Remade in the US as *The Magnificent Seven* (1960).

**Sunset Boulevard (1950, Billy Wilder)**
- Narrated by a dead man (Joe Gillis) floating in a pool — famous opening.
- Norma Desmond's line: "I'm ready for my close-up."

**Singin' in the Rain (1952)**
- Directed by/starring Gene Kelly and Stanley Donen; about Hollywood's transition from silent to sound films.

**Lawrence of Arabia (1962, David Lean)**
- Famous "match cut" from a blown-out match to a desert sunrise.
- Peter O'Toole played T.E. Lawrence.

**Psycho (1960, Alfred Hitchcock)**
- Shower scene score by Bernard Herrmann; Anthony Perkins as Norman Bates.

**The Rules of the Game (1939, Jean Renoir)**
- Jean Renoir is the son of painter Pierre-Auguste Renoir.

**Tokyo Story (1953, Yasujiro Ozu)**
- Known for Ozu's signature low "tatami-level" camera shots.

**Persona (1966, Ingmar Bergman)**
- Famous for the merged/split-face shot of its two lead actresses.

**Apocalypse Now (1979, Francis Ford Coppola)**
- Loosely based on Joseph Conrad's *Heart of Darkness*; notoriously chaotic Philippines shoot (documented in *Hearts of Darkness*).

**Taxi Driver (1976, Martin Scorsese)**
- Robert De Niro's "You talkin' to me?" is improvised; score by Bernard Herrmann (his last).

**Raging Bull (1980, Martin Scorsese)**
- Shot in black-and-white; De Niro gained ~60 lbs to play older Jake LaMotta.

**Goodfellas (1990, Martin Scorsese)**
- Famous single-take Steadicam shot through the Copacabana.

**Pulp Fiction (1994, Quentin Tarantino)**
- Non-linear structure; won the Palme d'Or at Cannes 1994.

**Star Wars (1977, George Lucas)**
- Originally released simply as *Star Wars*; "Episode IV: A New Hope" subtitle added later.

**Jaws (1975, Steven Spielberg)**
- Often called the first "summer blockbuster"; mechanical shark nicknamed "Bruce."

**Schindler's List (1993, Steven Spielberg)**
- Shot mostly in black-and-white except the girl in the red coat.

**Blade Runner (1982, Ridley Scott)**
- Based on Philip K. Dick's *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*.

**Do the Right Thing (1989, Spike Lee)**
- Set entirely on one block in Brooklyn on the hottest day of the summer.

**There Will Be Blood (2007, Paul Thomas Anderson)**
- Daniel Day-Lewis's "I drink your milkshake" line; loosely based on Upton Sinclair's *Oil!*.

**In the Mood for Love (2000, Wong Kar-wai)**
- Known for its restrained romance and use of Nat King Cole songs on the soundtrack.

**City of God (2002, Fernando Meirelles)**
- Set in Rio de Janeiro's favelas; largely non-professional cast.

**Parasite (2019, Bong Joon-ho)**
- First non-English-language film to win the Best Picture Oscar; also won the Palme d'Or the same year (rare double).

**No Country for Old Men (2007, Coen Brothers)**
- Based on Cormac McCarthy's novel; Javier Bardem's character Anton Chigurh uses a captive bolt pistol.

**Boyhood (2014, Richard Linklater)**
- Filmed over 12 years with the same cast to show the lead actor age in real time.

**Rocky (1976)**
- Written by and starred Sylvester Stallone; won Best Picture over Taxi Driver and Network.

**Delhi-6 (2009)**
- **[doc]** Frequently referenced in COQ-style quizzes; directed by Rakeysh Omprakash Mehra, features the "Kala Bandar" urban-legend subplot.

**General note [doc]:** Palme d'Or, Golden Lion (Venice), and Oscar winners are commonly asked — know recent years' winners. Recent films (Dune, an LoTR sequel, Marty Supreme, Hundreds of Beavers, etc.) may be quizzed on specific craft categories (production design, score, cinematography) rather than plot.

## Music

**Symphony No. 9 (Beethoven)**
- Premiered 1824; Beethoven was almost completely deaf by then. Final movement uses the "Ode to Joy" choral theme — now the anthem of the EU.

**The Well-Tempered Clavier (Bach)**
- A set of preludes and fugues in all 24 major/minor keys, demonstrating equal temperament tuning.

**The Rite of Spring (Stravinsky)**
- Its 1913 Paris premiere caused a near-riot due to its dissonance and choreography.

**The Magic Flute (Mozart)**
- A singspiel (German opera with spoken dialogue); Mozart's last opera, premiered 1791.

**The Blue Danube**
- **[doc]** Composed by Johann Strauss II; used memorably in *2001: A Space Odyssey*'s space-station docking sequence.

**Canon (song that uses it)**
- **[doc]** Refers to Pachelbel's Canon in D — one of the most sampled/borrowed classical pieces in pop music.

**Requiem (Mozart)**
- **[doc]** Left unfinished at Mozart's death; completed by his student Franz Xaver Süssmayr.
- **[doc]** "Lux Aeterna" is also the title of a Metallica arrangement/cover tied to Mozart's Requiem material (performed with the San Francisco Symphony on S&M2).

**The Four Seasons (Vivaldi)**
- A set of four violin concertos, each with an accompanying sonnet describing the season.

**Kind of Blue (Miles Davis, 1959)**
- Best-selling jazz album of all time; pioneered "modal jazz."

**Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (Beatles, 1967)**
- Considered one of the first "concept albums"; iconic collage album cover.

**Pet Sounds (Beach Boys, 1966)**
- Brian Wilson's response to the Beatles' *Rubber Soul*; inspired *Sgt. Pepper's*.

**Thriller (Michael Jackson, 1982)**
- Best-selling album of all time worldwide; title track's music video directed by John Landis.

**Abbey Road (Beatles, 1969)**
- Famous zebra-crossing cover shot outside Abbey Road Studios, London.

**The Dark Side of the Moon (Pink Floyd, 1973)**
- **[doc]** Album closer "Eclipse" ends with the spoken line "There is no dark side of the moon, really... it's all dark," spoken by Gerry O'Driscoll, the Irish doorman at Abbey Road Studios.
- **[doc]** The album's prism cover art and the "Dark Side of the Rainbow" myth (syncing it to *The Wizard of Oz*) are popular trivia; the "heartbeat" bookends the album (associations with Dalí/"melting time" imagery are sometimes drawn in trivia, though not official).

**What's Going On (Marvin Gaye, 1971)**
- A concept album responding to the Vietnam War and social injustice.

**Songs in the Key of Life (Stevie Wonder, 1976)**
- Double album; one of very few albums to debut at #1 on the Billboard 200.

**Nevermind (Nirvana, 1991)**
- "Smells Like Teen Spirit" led the album to dethrone Michael Jackson's *Dangerous* at #1.

**Rumours (Fleetwood Mac, 1977)**
- Written amid multiple band-member breakups (including Stevie Nicks/Lindsey Buckingham).

**Blue (Joni Mitchell, 1971)**
- Confessional singer-songwriter landmark; ranked among the greatest albums ever by multiple critics' polls.

**Purple Rain (Prince, 1984)**
- **[doc]** Stevie Nicks was reportedly asked by Prince to write lyrics to the title track but felt the piece was too overwhelming ("Purple Rain" trivia often ties Prince to Stevie Nicks this way).
- Both an album and a semi-autobiographical film starring Prince.

**OK Computer (Radiohead, 1997)**
- Concept themes of technology/alienation; often cited as a defining 90s alt-rock album.

**The Blueprint (Jay-Z, 2001)**
- Released September 11, 2001; introduced production talents Kanye West and Just Blaze to a wide audience.

**Lemonade (Beyoncé, 2016)**
- Visual album released with an accompanying HBO film; addresses infidelity/Black womanhood themes.

**Bohemian Rhapsody (Queen)**
- Six-minute suite with no repeating chorus; famously almost cut from *Wayne's World* car-headbanging scene (it wasn't, and it revived the song's popularity in 1992).

**Like a Rolling Stone (Bob Dylan)**
- Marked Dylan's controversial shift to electric rock (booed at Newport/Manchester "Judas!" heckle era).

**Imagine (John Lennon)**
- Written/recorded at his and Yoko Ono's home, Tittenhurst Park.

**Billie Jean (Michael Jackson)**
- Iconic light-up sidewalk music video; from *Thriller*.

**George Harrison / "The Boys of Dungeon Lane"**
- **[doc]** Reference ties to George Harrison, his estate Friar Park, and the L24 postcode area, plus the song "We Two" — niche Beatles/Harrison trivia about his home life and lesser-known recordings.

## Literature — Novels

**Don Quixote (Cervantes)**
- Often called the first modern novel; Quixote tilts at windmills believing them giants.

**War and Peace (Tolstoy)**
- Follows five aristocratic families during the Napoleonic Wars; famously long (~1,225 pages).

**Anna Karenina (Tolstoy)**
- Opens with: a line (paraphrased) about happy families all being alike, unhappy families each unhappy differently.

**Crime and Punishment (Dostoevsky)**
- Follows Raskolnikov, who murders a pawnbroker and grapples with guilt.

**Ulysses (James Joyce)**
- Set entirely on a single day, June 16, 1904 — celebrated annually as "Bloomsday."

**One Hundred Years of Solitude (García Márquez)**
- Landmark of magical realism; follows the Buendía family in the fictional town of Macondo.

**1984 (George Orwell)**
- Introduced terms like "Big Brother," "doublethink," and "Newspeak."

**To Kill a Mockingbird (Harper Lee)**
- Narrated by Scout Finch; her father Atticus defends a falsely accused Black man in Depression-era Alabama.

**The Great Gatsby (F. Scott Fitzgerald)**
- Narrated by Nick Carraway; the green light at the end of Daisy's dock is a famous symbol.

**Moby-Dick (Herman Melville)**
- Opens with "Call me Ishmael"; Captain Ahab hunts the white whale.

**Beloved (Toni Morrison)**
- Inspired by the true story of Margaret Garner, an enslaved woman; won the Pulitzer Prize.

**Midnight's Children (Salman Rushdie)**
- Protagonist Saleem Sinai is born at the exact moment of India's independence (midnight, Aug 15, 1947).

**In Search of Lost Time (Marcel Proust)**
- Famous for the "madeleine" memory scene; one of the longest novels ever written.

**The Brothers Karamazov (Dostoevsky)**
- Dostoevsky's final novel; centers on a patricide and three (or four) brothers' philosophical/moral conflicts.

## Literature — Poetry and Epic

**The Odyssey / The Iliad (Homer)**
- The Iliad covers the Trojan War (Achilles' wrath); the Odyssey follows Odysseus's ten-year journey home.

**The Divine Comedy (Dante)**
- Divided into Inferno, Purgatorio, Paradiso; guided through Hell by the poet Virgil.

**Leaves of Grass (Walt Whitman)**
- Whitman revised/expanded it across his lifetime; free-verse, celebrates the self and democracy.

**The Waste Land (T.S. Eliot)**
- Opens with "April is the cruellest month"; heavily annotated/fragmented modernist poem.

**[doc] General:** Booker Prize and Nobel Prize in Literature winners (recent years) are common quiz fodder — worth reviewing the last 5 years of each.

## Plays

**Hamlet (Shakespeare)**
- Famous "To be or not to be" soliloquy; Prince of Denmark avenges his father's murder.

**Oedipus Rex (Sophocles)**
- Oedipus unknowingly kills his father and marries his mother, fulfilling a prophecy.

**A Streetcar Named Desire (Tennessee Williams)**
- Stanley Kowalski's "STELLA!" cry is one of the most quoted moments in American theatre.

**Death of a Salesman (Arthur Miller)**
- Willy Loman is the tragic everyman salesman; critiques the American Dream.

**Waiting for Godot (Samuel Beckett)**
- Two characters, Vladimir and Estragon, wait for a man named Godot who never arrives — a landmark of the Theatre of the Absurd.

**A Doll's House (Henrik Ibsen)**
- Ends with Nora Helmer famously walking out on her husband and children ("the door slam heard around the world").

**The Importance of Being Earnest (Oscar Wilde)**
- A farce hinging on two men both pretending to be named "Ernest."

**Angels in America (Tony Kushner)**
- Two-part epic about AIDS in 1980s America; won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama.

**Macbeth / King Lear / The Merchant of Venice (Shakespeare)**
- Macbeth: prophecy from three witches drives regicide and downfall.
- King Lear: king divides his kingdom based on flattery, banishing his most honest daughter Cordelia.
- The Merchant of Venice: Shylock's "pound of flesh" bond is the central plot device.

## Shows

**Breaking Bad / Better Call Saul**
- Walter White's alias is "Heisenberg"; both shows are set in Albuquerque, New Mexico (Vince Gilligan).

**The Wire**
- Set in Baltimore; known for its multi-season focus on different institutions (drugs, ports, schools, media, politics).

**The Sopranos**
- Tony Soprano is a New Jersey mob boss who sees a therapist, Dr. Melfi; famous cut-to-black finale.

**Fleabag**
- Written by/starring Phoebe Waller-Bridge; known for its fourth-wall-breaking direct-camera address.

**Derry Girls**
- Set in Derry, Northern Ireland during the Troubles in the 1990s.

**Seinfeld**
- Billed as "a show about nothing"; co-created by Jerry Seinfeld and Larry David.

**Friends**
- Set in New York; the coffeehouse is "Central Perk."

**Game of Thrones**
- Based on George R. R. Martin's *A Song of Ice and Fire* novels.

**I Love Lucy**
- Pioneered the multi-camera sitcom format and filming before a live studio audience.

**The Simpsons**
- Longest-running American scripted primetime series; set in the fictional Springfield.

**Mad Men**
- Set in a 1960s Madison Avenue ad agency; protagonist Don Draper has a hidden past identity.

**The Office (UK original)**
- **[doc]** Created by/starring Ricky Gervais and Stephen Merchant; mockumentary format later adapted into the US version.

**Chernobyl (miniseries)**
- Dramatizes the 1986 nuclear disaster in the Soviet Union; HBO/Sky co-production.

**Succession**
- Follows the Roy family's media empire (Waystar Royco), loosely echoing real media dynasties.

**Sandman**
- **[doc]** Based on Neil Gaiman's comics; main character is Dream/Morpheus (often a quiz-asked detail).

**1899**
- Netflix mystery series from the creators of *Dark*; canceled after one season.

**Pluribus**
- Vince Gilligan's Apple TV+ sci-fi series (premiered Nov 7, 2025), starring Rhea Seehorn; centers on an alien virus creating a peaceful hive-mind, with a handful of immune people, including novelist Carol Sturka.

## Video Games

**Tetris**
- Created by Soviet programmer Alexey Pajitnov in 1984.

**Super Mario Bros. / Super Mario 64**
- Super Mario Bros. (1985) is credited with reviving the US video game industry after the 1983 crash; Super Mario 64 (1996) pioneered 3D platforming and camera control.

**The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time / Breath of the Wild**
- Ocarina of Time (1998) introduced Z-targeting (lock-on combat); Breath of the Wild (2017) redefined open-world exploration for the series.

**Half-Life / Half-Life 2**
- Protagonist is Gordon Freeman, a theoretical physicist; Half-Life 2's Gravity Gun is iconic.

**Minecraft**
- Created by Markus "Notch" Persson; best-selling video game of all time.

**Pac-Man**
- Created by Toru Iwatani (Namco, 1980); ghosts are named Blinky, Pinky, Inky, and Clyde.

**Doom (1993)**
- Pioneered the first-person shooter genre and early LAN deathmatch play.

**Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2**
- Frequently ranked among the greatest sports/skateboarding games ever made.

**Portal 2**
- Sequel to Portal; features GLaDOS and the "companion cube"; praised for puzzle design and writing.

**Red Dead Redemption 2**
- Rockstar Games' open-world Western, prequel to the original RDR; follows outlaw Arthur Morgan.

**The Last of Us**
- Naughty Dog's post-apocalyptic survival game; follows Joel and Ellie, adapted into an HBO series.

**Grand Theft Auto V / VI**
- **[doc]** GTA V (2013) is one of the best-selling entertainment products of all time; GTA VI is highly anticipated with a 2020s release window.

## Important Artists (Directors / Movements)

**Orson Welles**
- Directed *Citizen Kane*, *The Magnificent Ambersons*, *Touch of Evil*, *F for Fake*; also famous for the 1938 "War of the Worlds" radio broadcast panic.

**Stanley Kubrick**
- Known for perfectionism/many takes; directed 2001, The Shining, A Clockwork Orange, Dr. Strangelove.

**Alfred Hitchcock**
- "Master of Suspense"; famous for cameo appearances in his own films.

**Jean Renoir**
- Son of Impressionist painter Pierre-Auguste Renoir; directed *The Rules of the Game* and *Grand Illusion*.

**Gregg Toland**
- Cinematographer on *Citizen Kane*; pioneered deep-focus photography.

**Akira Kurosawa**
- Japanese director; *Seven Samurai*, *Rashomon* (gave rise to the term "Rashomon effect").

**Ingmar Bergman**
- Swedish director; *The Seventh Seal*, *Persona*, frequent collaborator with cinematographer Sven Nykvist.

**Federico Fellini**
- Italian director; *La Dolce Vita*, *8½* — gave us the adjective "Felliniesque."

**Martin Scorsese**
- Frequent collaborator with Robert De Niro and later Leonardo DiCaprio; Catholic guilt and NYC crime are recurring themes.

**Paul Thomas Anderson**
- *Boogie Nights*, *Magnolia*, *There Will Be Blood*, *Phantom Thread*.

**Richard Linklater**
- Known for the "Before" trilogy and *Boyhood*'s 12-year shoot.

**Michelangelo Antonioni**
- Italian director of "alienation trilogy" (*L'Avventura*, *La Notte*, *L'Eclisse*).

**Godard, Truffaut, Varda, Demy — French New Wave**
- Jean-Luc Godard (*Breathless*), François Truffaut (*The 400 Blows*), Agnès Varda (*Cléo from 5 to 7*), Jacques Demy (*The Umbrellas of Cherbourg*) — core figures of the French New Wave (late 1950s–60s).

**Ray, Ghatak, Sinha, Kaul, Kashyap, Ali, Basu (Indian directors)**
- Satyajit Ray: *The Apu Trilogy*, India's most internationally celebrated director.
- Ritwik Ghatak: *Meghe Dhaka Tara* (Cloud-Capped Star); Partition themes.
- Tapan Sinha: *Ek Doctor Ki Maut* — medical-establishment critique.
- Mani Kaul: key figure of Indian Parallel/New Wave Cinema.
- Anurag Kashyap: *Gangs of Wasseypur*.
- Imtiaz Ali: known for romantic road-trip narratives (*Jab We Met*, *Rockstar*).
- Anurag Basu: *Barfi!*, *Life in a Metro*.

**Ari Aster**
- Known for *Hereditary*, *Midsommar*, *Beau Is Afraid* — modern elevated horror.

**Indian film actors (as a group) [doc]**
- Naseeruddin Shah, Om Puri, Smita Patil, Shabana Azmi — pillars of Indian Parallel Cinema.
- Manoj Bajpayee, Irrfan Khan, Nawazuddin Siddiqui, Pankaj Tripathi, Kay Kay Menon, Jaideep Ahlawat — associated with the post-2000s "new realism" wave (often *Gangs of Wasseypur*-adjacent).

## Indian Artists (Painters/Sculptors)

**Amrita Sher-Gil**
- Hungarian-Indian painter, often called India's Frida Kahlo; died young (age 28).

**Raja Ravi Varma**
- Known for depicting Hindu deities/mythological scenes in a European academic realist style; pioneered oleograph mass reproduction.

**Abanindranath Tagore**
- Founder of the Bengal School of Art; nephew of Rabindranath Tagore.

**Rabindranath Tagore**
- Nobel laureate (Literature, 1913, *Gitanjali*) who also took up painting later in life.

**M. F. Husain**
- Co-founder of the Progressive Artists' Group; known as the "Picasso of India."

**Francis Newton Souza**
- Founder of the Progressive Artists' Group.

**Sayed Haider Raza (S. H. Raza)**
- Known for the "Bindu" (dot/circle) motif in his abstract work.

**Tyeb Mehta**
- Known for the recurring "falling figure" and "Kali" motifs; his paintings have set Indian auction records.

**Nandalal Bose**
- Bengal School artist; illustrated the original Constitution of India.

**Anish Kapoor**
- Sculptor known for *Cloud Gate* ("The Bean") in Chicago; controversial exclusive rights to "Vantablack."

**Subodh Gupta**
- Contemporary artist known for using everyday utensils (steel tiffin boxes, thalis) in large-scale sculpture.
